# Math Kangaroo Worlds: spiral curriculum proposal

**For sequence review — 22 September 2026. Not an approved runtime manifest.**

The proposed road is **Counting 1 → Symmetry 1 → Addition & Subtraction 1 →
Patterns 1 → the other concept worlds → Counting 2 → Symmetry 2 → …**.
Each world has one primary concept. Returning to that concept introduces a
harder reasoning move, rather than merely larger numbers or a new backdrop.

The recommendation is **four spiral passes, with 16 recurring concept worlds**:
64 core world slots. Six additional Possibilities and Chance & Data slots are
conditional on recovering enough suitable questions. These are 70 proposed
slots, **not 70 assembled or verified question banks**. The point of this file
is to make the entire sequence reviewable before producing those worlds.

The playable Counting Coast remains an **art and navigation prototype using its
existing mixed bank**. Its stop names are not trustworthy skill assignments:
for example, its Clock Tower currently contains animal-weight and exchange
questions. This proposal includes a separate, counting-only first-world bank
below; it does not silently replace the playable questions or saved progress.

## The four passes

| Pass | Purpose | Initial corpus search pool | Change in reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Notice | Establish the concept | Grades 1–2 | Concrete pictures, one main relation, generous distinctions |
| 2 · Connect | Apply it in another representation | Grades 3–4 | Two related steps, missing information, plausible near-matches |
| 3 · Plan | Choose and organize a strategy | Grades 5–6 | Interacting constraints, cases, decomposition, transfer |
| 4 · Generalize | Explain why a strategy works | Grades 7–12 | Structural arguments, invariants, algebraic or geometric generalization |

These bands are **search pools**, not age gates, ability labels, or a claim that
every Grade 3–4 question is harder than every Grade 1–2 question. A visual
Grade 3–4 question can be an excellent earlier fit; a Grade 1–2 five-point
question can require substantially more reasoning than a later easy item.
Published point tiers and question numbers are secondary sorting signals only.

Pass 4 spans a wide range. Its entrance uses accessible Grades 7–8 problems;
Grades 9–12 supply later optional stretch routes only when their prerequisites
have already appeared. Do not force an advanced item into the road just to use
the oldest grade band.

The existing `concrete → direct → structured → integrated → novel` authoring
stages describe questions *within* these passes. They remain separate from
player-facing concept numbers. The existing six realms remain useful
catalogue folders; the proposed world unit is a concept, superseding the old
two-to-four-realms-per-world plan only if this sequence is accepted.

## Proposed order and progression

Read down a column to play a pass, then return to the top of the next column.
The table defines the whole proposed core sequence, not just the first chapter.

| Order | Concept world | 1 · Notice | 2 · Connect | 3 · Plan | 4 · Generalize |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Counting** | Count once; filter objects; infer missing units | Groups, overlaps, inclusive endpoints | Cases and complements | Justify counts with structure and invariants |
| 2 | **Symmetry** | Mirror halves and one reflection | Folds, axes, turns versus mirrors | Compose transformations; preserved features | Use symmetry to reduce cases and prove relations |
| 3 | **Addition & Subtraction** | Join, remove, compare | Inverses and two-step comparisons | Efficient decomposition; signed quantities | Expressions and additive invariants |
| 4 | **Patterns** | Repeating units; simple growth | Missing positions and cycles | Recursive versus position rules | Generalize and justify a rule |
| 5 | **Shape Building** | Boundaries and fitting pieces | Decompose and tile | Angles, congruence, similarity | Geometric arguments and useful constructions |
| 6 | **Logic** | Combine two clues | Order, place, work backward | Interacting constraints and cases | Contradiction, invariants, exhaustive reasoning |
| 7 | **Groups & Sharing** | Equal groups and fair sharing | Multiplication, division, remainders | Ratios and unit rates | Changing rates and proportional constraints |
| 8 | **Number & Digits** | Place value and digit composition | Parity and simple divisibility | Factors, multiples, remainders | Modular structure, factorization, powers |
| 9 | **Routes & Grids** | Positions, steps, visited points | Constrained grid routes | Route counting and connectivity | Coordinate and graph arguments |
| 10 | **Fractions** | Equal parts and pictured halves | Compare, compose, reconstruct a whole | Operations, decimals, percentages | Successive changes and fractional constraints |
| 11 | **Solids & Views** | Simple views and visible blocks | Hidden blocks, nets, multiple views | Sections, painted faces, surface and volume | Spatial decomposition and proof |
| 12 | **Length & Measure** | Compare and repeat equal units | Combine measures; interpret scale | Units, scale, capacity, distance | Indirect measurement and modeling |
| 13 | **Money & Value** | Coins and small purchases | Change and equivalent purchases | Exchanges, bundles, proportional prices | Optimize purchases under constraints |
| 14 | **Time** | Clocks, calendars, short intervals | Elapsed time across boundaries | Schedules, periodic events, travel | Simultaneous cycles and rates |
| 15 | **Area & Boundary** | Whole-square coverage and boundary tracing* | Area versus perimeter | Composite and shaded regions | Area relations and constrained boundaries |
| 16 | **Missing Values** | One pictured balance or equality | Linked equalities and codes | Coupled unknowns and equations | Systems, functions, algebraic structure |

\* **Area & Boundary 1 is a proposed slot with a retrieval gap.** Only five
Grade 1–2 records currently carry area/perimeter tags. First inspect the
shape-building and counting pools for genuine unit-coverage questions; if that
does not yield a coherent world, defer its first appearance to pass 2 and
renumber that concept. Do not stretch the concept or repeat questions to fill
a quota.

After Missing Values in passes 2–4, consider two specialist worlds:

| Pass | Possibilities | Chance & Data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | **Possibilities 1:** systematic lists and choice combinations | **Chance & Data 1:** simple displays and equally likely outcomes |
| 3 | **Possibilities 2:** products, organized cases, complementary counting | **Chance & Data 2:** compare probabilities and aggregate data |
| 4 | **Possibilities 3:** permutations, combinations, inclusion-exclusion | **Chance & Data 3:** conditional information and structured sample spaces |

These six are conditional, especially Chance & Data. The present classifier
finds too few explicit candidates to commit them to the required road. Their
absence from an early release should not lock players out of the next spiral.

The longer-term curriculum covers the six existing realms without demanding
equal numbers of worlds per realm. Closely connected advanced concepts remain
in their appropriate strands: ratios in Groups & Sharing, divisibility in
Number & Digits, work backward in Logic, and algebra in Missing Values. If
playtesting shows a strand becomes too broad, split that advanced world after
the concepts have been introduced, while retaining stable semantic IDs.

## Inside one concept world

Start with **12 main questions in four short stops of three questions**:

1. **Recognize:** three variations on the core action.
2. **Organize:** introduce one useful representation or strategy.
3. **Apply:** vary the context without changing the target concept.
4. **Summit:** three fresh problems requiring the same concept more independently.

A world can grow to 18–24 questions when the bank contains distinct,
instructionally useful variations. A scarce concept can have a shorter map.
Question volume is not a reason to add unrelated problems. Optional minigames
and Turbo branches remain separate from the required reasoning road.

Within a world, order by actual solution demands: number of relations, number
of cases, hidden structure, required representation changes, and prerequisite
operations. Keep incidental reading and arithmetic load small where they are
not the concept. Alternate easy consolidation with modest increases; the
last item need not be the most obscure contest puzzle.

At the next numbered world, begin with a fresh retrieval problem from the
previous concept level, then extend it. Mistake redemption and optional
return visits supply additional practice between passes. Existing first-try
history must remain unchanged by retries. New main-road items must not repeat
an earlier problem or a translated/source-duplicate version of it.

The important gaps and consequences are:

- **Symmetry:** only 41 explicit candidates across all bands. Inspect the wider
  transformation pools for true reflection and folding problems; unrelated
  rotations do not become symmetry questions by renaming them. Use a shorter
  first map or defer a slot if the real pool is too small.
- **Early equal groups and missing values:** the current player supports five
  choices. Groups & Sharing 1 has only 11 five-choice candidates, and Missing
  Values 3 has only seven. Either structure the existing four-choice items
  properly or select from neighboring bands based on reasoning demands.
- **Patterns, money, possibilities:** several pools have fewer than 24
  candidates before deduplication and true-concept checks. Prefer a compact
  world to a padded one.
- **Chance/data and early area:** sparse detection is a classification gap,
  not evidence that the subject is unimportant or absent. Search the diagrams
  and source families directly, then retain or defer those proposed slots.
- **Language and source presentation:** some complete-choice records are not
  English-only, or retain source choices inside images. Inspect the actual
  player-facing presentation before selection; metadata alone is insufficient.

## What acceptance would authorize next

The useful review decisions are the repeating concept order, whether 12
questions makes a satisfying first concept world, and the four-pass scope.
The aesthetics can be evaluated now on the independent first-world prototype.

After sequence and aesthetics acceptance:

1. Assign concept owners in parallel and inspect actual questions, using the
   counts and references as retrieval leads. Keep the first Counting bank
   separate until its source and presentation details are resolved.
2. Give every chosen problem exactly one primary home, bind its content
   version, detect cross-source duplicates, and order by solution demands.
3. Freeze small world manifests with explicit prerequisite and coverage notes.
   Resolve thin or empty slots honestly; no unrelated fallback questions.
4. Produce concept-specific maps in parallel from the accepted visual system,
   preserving the same readable roads, native controls, and test-user access.
5. Playtest difficulty jumps, misconception feedback, source answer mapping,
   mobile diagrams, retries, history, and resumption before treating a world
   as a finished curriculum unit.

This proposal does not change Journey boards, canonical game Campaigns, XP,
the approved skill ontology, or the existing world manifest. No future world
has been implemented or locked into progression by this work.

## Reproduce the evidence

Run the read-only corpus report from the repository root:

```sh
python3 scripts/draft-math-world-spiral.py
```

It reads the explicitly pinned catalogue using SQLite `mode=ro` and writes
[`content/math-world/spiral-curriculum.proposed.json`](../content/math-world/spiral-curriculum.proposed.json).
That file contains all 70 ordered slots, four-pass objectives, retrieval tags,
four/five-choice counts, sample item references, and the exact first-world
proposal with content hashes. It contains no prompts, answers, source URLs,
or private asset paths and is not imported by the application.
