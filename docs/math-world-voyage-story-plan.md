# Oceania shard voyage — authoring plan

Updated: 2026-10-12. Status: implemented and validated for release.

## Scope and continuity

The voyage is an optional side story for Counting Coast. Math Kangaroo practice
remains the main activity. The story's only teaching purpose is introducing a
small amount of maritime and mythological vocabulary through the existing
glossary.

The existing welcome, two first-world books, sundering, shard flight, and appeal
remain unchanged. A student who has completed the first archipelago sees the
same opening history. New chapter 01 follows the existing appeal and recovers
the first shard at that already visited summit. It does not replay practice or
require another completion.

The Tideheart remains the scientists' beneficial crystal weather computer,
introduced by the original opening. It does not become an ancient god or a
fallen star. Its familiar blue signals are the basis for the later discovery
that it can protect the islands from a distance.

There are exactly thirty-two shards, one per archipelago summit. Chapters
01–32 each recover their own summit shard in the prose. The two final chapters
recover no additional shards. No shard is stolen, lost, or tied to a correct
answer, score, test, printed packet, or redemption outcome.

## Reading contract

- The first archipelago keeps its two original books.
- Archipelagos 2–32 have one optional voyage book each.
- The new chapters form a single ordered manuscript available through an
  explicit **Continue story** control, independently of math progression.
- Reading, skipping, closing, or rereading story pages never changes curriculum
  access, question state, accuracy, mistakes, workbooks, or earned progress.
- Shard acknowledgements describe the fiction's reading progress; they are not
  puzzle rewards or requirements for practice.
- Hurricanes in chapters 16 and 32 are narrative weather. Characters take
  shelter and wait. Assessments and their packets remain separate activities;
  completing one does not defeat a storm or power the Tideheart.
- Each chapter has two short paragraphs. Major plot turns occur at a few ports;
  the other visits provide scenery, small acts of kindness, and breathing room.
- There are no new reading-comprehension checks, vocabulary quizzes, combat
  encounters, timed decisions, or other minigames.

## The Veiled Compass

Before the Tideheart, the Veiled Compass controlled a naturally sheltered strait
through the dangerous sea. Captains paid tolls to use it. The computer opened
safe routes between every island, and the guild lost its monopoly.

The guild caused the existing sundering and now wants every shard destroyed.
Its leaders want dangerous seas to return so sailors will need their paid
passage. This motive explains their sabotage without inventing hidden faults,
earlier misuse, or a new origin for the beneficial Tideheart.

The covered-compass emblem appears before the group's name and purpose are
revealed. The guild is not present at every port. Mara, a guild sailor rescued
in chapter 19, has seen the damage caused by the storms and chooses to help.
Her change of course is brief and voluntary. She shares local knowledge and
warns the islanders of the final approach; she neither betrays the player nor
removes collected shards.

The final solution is practical within the fantasy: keep the working weather
computer aboard a moving celestial ship, beyond the saboteurs' reach, while its
signals continue to protect all Oceania. Ordinary islanders first escort the
ship safely past the approaching guild. No battle is required.

## Odyssey echoes and tone

The Cyclops lighthouse, Aeolus-like wind bags, and Siren's welcoming song are
compact, friendly echoes rather than retellings of the Odyssey. Each is one
visit, with no extra quest system or mythological knowledge required. Other
stops use ordinary local needs rather than turning every resident into a source
of clues.

The tone is curious and reassuring. Storms create a reason to restore the
computer, but shelter is available and nobody must hurry. The ending gives the
player a continuing voyage and a visible connection to home. It does not impose
lonely exile, remove access to the islands, or claim that practice mistakes
place Oceania in danger.

## Vocabulary

`VOYAGE_STORY_GLOSSARY` adds twenty-one terms that appear in the authored paragraphs:
Aeolus, Atoll, Beacon, Celestial, Chart, Compass, Constellation, Cyclops, Fjord,
Haven, Mooring, Prow, Quay, Rigging, Siren, Stern, Strait, Sundering, Tiller, Toll, and
Windward. Definitions are short, single-sentence field-guide entries and reveal
no plot outcomes. The existing glossary remains the single vocabulary surface;
the manuscript does not interrupt itself with definitions.

## Content API and validation

`app/math-world/voyage-story-content.ts` exports:

- `VoyageChapter`, a readonly `StoryPageContent` with `worldNumber` and optional
  `shardWorld`.
- `VOYAGE_CHAPTERS`, the ordered 32 recovery chapters and two finale chapters.
- `VOYAGE_STORY_GLOSSARY`, the twenty-one grounded vocabulary additions.

Recovery IDs are stable: `oceania-voyage-01` through `oceania-voyage-32`.
Finale IDs are `oceania-voyage-reforging` and `oceania-voyage-ascent`.
Illustration kinds are `chart`, `beacon`, `wind`, `sails`, `reforging`, and
`ascent`, with `welcome`, `peace`, and `appeal` reused where appropriate.

Content checks should establish ordered unique IDs, exactly one recovery for
each summit, no additional finale shards, short two-paragraph chapters,
glossary grounding and concise definitions. Integration checks should establish
that independent story reading, map book access, history, resume, and shard
display never change practice state, and that the existing first-world opening
and saved student progress stay intact.

Release validation passed: `npm run check` (lint, types, static export, 1,106
JavaScript tests with eight existing skips, 174 adaptive-core tests, and ten
search tests) and six Chromium story checks. Browser coverage includes all
34 chapters, saved progress and reloads, the unchanged opening, optional books,
glossary layouts at 390/620/820/1200px, keyboard focus, paused scenery, and reduced
motion changes during both finales. These browser checks use the supported
static map fallback on the GPU-less runner; the new story illustrations are SVG.
